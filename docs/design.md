# Design

The target state for Yiju. This is not a description of the current code, which
differs substantially. Where the two disagree, this document is the intent and
the code is the thing that needs to change.

## The problem

Chinese is morphosyllabic. One syllable is one character, but a word is one to
four characters and a character is not necessarily a word. 爸 never appears
alone; it is a bound morpheme that only occurs inside words like 爸爸, 阿爸, 老爸.
Single-character words exist (人, 水, 大) but a learner cannot predict which is
which. Learning is therefore a word-level activity, and a word is best learned
in a sentence rather than from a definition.

The app exists to make that automatic. The user supplies words and sentences;
the app decides what to show and when, in a way that never needs the user to
sort their own material by hand.

## Core model

Everything is a graph. Words are nodes. Sentences are the edges that connect
them. Nothing is chosen explicitly; the graph assembles itself from a handful
of seeds.

### Word states

Four states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| locked | In the library, but not reachable by any eligible sentence |
| unlocked | Reachable by an eligible sentence, or opened manually |
| learning | A card containing this word has been studied; review strength is still low |
| learned | Review strength has crossed the known threshold |

`learning` and `learned` both satisfy "seen". They are distinguished only by how
strong the review scheduling is behind them.

### Sentence states

Three states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| locked | More than two words in it are unseen |
| eligible | At most two words in it are unseen |
| unlocked | Every word in it has been seen; the sentence is shown |

An eligible sentence is never displayed. Its words unlock, and the words enter
the study queue. Once every word in the sentence is seen, the sentence itself
unlocks and joins the queue.

### "Seen"

A word is seen when one of its own cards has been studied. This is unambiguous
because a sentence containing an unseen word cannot be unlocked, so the sentence
cannot be shown, so the word cannot be seen through it. A word's own card is the
only route to it becoming seen.

Seen is one-way. A word that has been seen is never relocked, even if it later
regresses from `learned` to `learning`.

### Transitions

A word unlocks only by appearing in an eligible sentence. Manual unlock is
available as an override and is how a fresh install bootstraps: with every
sentence locked there is nothing studyable, so the user opens the first few
words by hand and the graph grows from there.

Relock is automatic and flows backwards. A word that is unlocked only because of
one sentence returns to locked if that sentence loses eligibility, which happens
when a word inside it regresses from `learned` to `learning`. If several
sentences could have supplied the word, losing one is not enough; it relocks
only when it has no remaining source. Relock never applies to a word that has
been seen.

Manual unlock applies to words only. There is no way to force a sentence
unlocked, because doing so would show words the user has not seen.

### Study order

Reviews always precede new material. The daily cap counts new *words*, not
cards. The cap is a plain setting the user changes whenever they like; nothing
adapts it automatically. Sentences unlocked by those words ride along and are
not counted against the cap. If reviews alone exhaust the day, that is
acceptable; reviews still happen and new words wait.

New words are drawn at random from the set of unlocked, unseen words at the
moment they are presented, not from a stored queue. A stored list would fix an
order in advance, and that order would go stale the instant a word relocked
underneath it.

## Content sources

Definitions come from CC-CEDICT. Sentences come from outside the app, either
hand-written or generated by an LLM in a separate pass. The app contains no
sentence generator.

### CC-CEDICT

Free, no hosting, no rate limit, updated daily. Format is
`TRAD SIMP [pinyin] /gloss/`. Note that field 1 is traditional and field 2 is
simplified; 47,356 of 125,297 entries are simplified-only, so lookup must try
both columns rather than assuming a mapping exists. Line endings are CRLF.

Verified: all 120 words of the current Pleco export resolve against CC-CEDICT,
including every reduplication and compound (爸爸, 妈妈, 同学, 打电话).

Limitations:

- No part of speech. Do not infer one; a wrong label is worse than none.
- Senses are slash-separated, not structured. Formatting at import has to split
  them and surface the parenthetical usage notes, which are the most useful
  content in the file. 得 gives four pronunciations with unrelated meanings
  across them; 是 is variously verb, conjunction and adjective.
- Readings per word are manageable at beginner level. Across a 120-word list,
  93 words have exactly one reading, 21 have two, and only 回 has four.

Attribution is required under CC-BY-SA and belongs on the About page.

### Sentences

The sentence pool is the curriculum. Every sentence in it can eventually become
studyable, so the pool determines what gets taught. There is no mechanism for the
app to introduce a word nobody wrote a sentence for.

On import, a sentence shows the words it introduces with their definitions. This
is a segmentation check: 葡萄很甜 might be two words or one word plus a
particle, and the display is where the user catches that. Words are offered
individually rather than as a bulk accept, because one stray typo in a sentence
would otherwise enter the library permanently.

## Cards

A word has two cards: characters to meaning, and meaning to characters. Both
backs show every reading. Sentences have two cards as well, one per direction.

Card types must be extensible. Adding a type should not require touching the
scheduling, queue, or study code. A candidate for later is character ordering:
given the characters and the meaning, reconstruct the sentence. It tests word
order and recognition rather than recall. Not implemented, recorded here because
the card model should not foreclose it.

Card review state is per card and independent of the sentence or word it belongs
to. Attribution from a sentence to its words is handled by the graph, not by
blending scheduling between the two.

## Library views

The library buckets words by state, plus one view worth naming: words not yet
present in any sentence. That is a backlog, and it is the honest answer to "what
do I learn next" when nothing is eligible.

## Why the current implementation is being replaced

Findings from analysing the Pleco export (120 words, 1,068 examples):

- **373 of 1,068 examples were silently discarded.** The import required the
  headword to appear as a standalone token, so sentences like 浓茶, 回家, 账本
  were dropped when the target word sat inside a larger word. Only 2 of the 373
  genuinely lacked the headword.
- **21 words had no examples at all.** 苹果 is `noun apple (the tree and its
  fruit)`. These are the easiest words in the list and they can only ever be
  bare word cards.
- **The unlocked-but-not-yet-learned concept does not exist.** Today a sentence
  is gated on words that are fully `known` by stability and retention
  thresholds, and words cannot be introduced by a sentence at all. The import
  rejects any sentence containing a word outside the vocabulary, so sentences
  can only ever use words added by hand.

Pleco itself is being dropped. Its parts-of-speech tagging was decorative: 不 had
eight senses all tagged `adverb`, including one that describes forming an
adjective from a noun.

## Resolved

These were open during design and are now settled. They are recorded because
the reasoning is easy to undo by accident.

**Sentence fan-out needs no special handling.** A word may appear in many
sentences and all of them may become eligible at once. This is harmless: the
resulting words are simply added to the pool of unlocked, unseen words and are
still drawn at random against the daily cap. There is no per-sentence ordering
or difficulty.

**An unlocked word is studyable whether or not a sentence will teach it.**
Unlocked means it is in the new-words pool. It does not imply that a sentence
containing it will unlock. Since a sentence can only unlock once all its words
are seen, the two cases do not conflict: an unlocked word with no eligible
sentence is simply one that was unlocked manually, and it is taught from its
own cards.
